## 1. Recovered supplemental evidence clusters

A. Dialogue / oral language / writing
- Frequent parent-child dialogue as a precursor to writing.
- Record the learner explaining a story/problem and preserve the learner's own words.
- Re-organize unclear speech by asking clarifying questions rather than replacing the child's voice.
- Reading/writing ability framed as a result of repeated conversation and explanation.

B. Understanding / thinking / memory
- Connect new knowledge to existing knowledge or personal experience.
- Prefer understanding → thinking/connection → retrieval/memory over isolated rote repetition.
- Use multimodal cues and meaningful associations when useful.
- Short, repeated retrieval is preferred over long one-shot drilling for retention.

C. Mathematics / problem solving
- Observe and ask questions before calculating.
- Find mathematics in everyday contexts.
- Ask the learner to explain the solution process in their own words.
- Treat errors as evidence of thinking, not merely as wrong answers.

D. One-line concept expression
- Read one concept, answer a thought question, then express one sentence in the learner's own words.
- Useful for economics/social concepts and transferable to other subjects.

E. Hanja / vocabulary semantic maps
- One anchor character/semantic root connects to multiple real words.
- Meaning, form, familiar usage and image/context cues can form a semantic neighborhood.
- Root-character maps are useful retrieval structures, but source vocabulary/illustrations are not canonical data.

F. Learning App Family / session evidence
- Existing family model reinforced: Ready & Set orchestrates one goal/session; Hide & Seek specializes in vocabulary discovery/retrieval; Snap & Pop specializes in expression/language use.
- Existing Goal Session + task LAP model is reinforced, not a new Learning Material atom.
- Existing internal-vs-child-facing rule is reinforced: engine terminology may remain internal while child UX uses find/hint/discover/recall language.

## 3. Atom definitions

### LRN-UTIL-013 — DIALOGUE_AS_COGNITIVE_SCAFFOLD
Use dialogue before or during output to surface ideas, sequence events, clarify meaning and lower blank-page friction.
Do not turn this into adult-authored text; preserve learner authorship.

### LRN-UTIL-014 — CONCEPT_TO_PERSONAL_EXPERIENCE
When a concept is abstract, connect it to a learner's prior experience or familiar situation before asking for formal expression.
Examples in source are evidence only; generate original contexts.

### LRN-UTIL-015 — UNDERSTAND_CONNECT_RETRIEVE_CHAIN
Learning flow should prefer:
1. understand meaning/context,
2. connect with prior knowledge or another representation,
3. retrieve later from memory.
Rote recall can still be used for facts that genuinely require exact recall, but it is not the sole learning strategy.

### LRN-UTIL-016 — PROCESS_VERBALIZATION_BEFORE_FINAL
For mathematics/problem solving, ask what the learner noticed, what is being asked, and how they would solve it before or alongside final-answer entry.
This extends QUESTION_BEFORE_CALCULATION + PROBLEM_RESTATE without transferring expression ownership away from Snap.

### LRN-UTIL-017 — REAL_WORLD_CONCEPT_DISCOVERY
Expose concepts through observable situations (quantity, comparison, pattern, slope, signal, market choice, etc.) before or alongside formal notation where pedagogically appropriate.

### LRN-UTIL-018 — SHORT_FREQUENT_RETRIEVAL
Use brief repeated retrieval/re-encounter loops rather than depending on one long memorization block.
Hide owns retrieval experience; Ready decides when/how it is routed into the session.

### LRN-UTIL-019 — ROOT_SEMANTIC_VOCABULARY_MAP
Build a meaning neighborhood around a root/character:
form/root → core meaning → related words → familiar context/image → retrieval cue → reuse.
For Hanja, grade/level authority stays with Ready Learning Engine; Hide owns memory/meaning experience.

### LRN-UTIL-020 — ONE_LINE_CONCEPT_EXPRESSION
After a concept/question, ask the learner to produce a short original sentence in their own words before expanding.
This is a small-output bridge into Snap's wider expression graph.

### LRN-UTIL-021 — MOTIVATION_FROM_EVIDENCE_NOT_GENDER
Do not personalize by hard-coded gender assumptions.
Adaptive motivation may use learner-chosen interests, observed persistence, explicit preferences, success history, task difficulty response and guardian-confirmed context.
